# CS 255 Portfolio – DriverPass

## Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?

DriverPass was a driver training company that wanted a web-based system to help students prepare for their driving exams. The system needed to allow customers to purchase training packages, take practice tests, schedule driving lessons, and track their progress. It also needed to give employees tools to manage customers, instructors, vehicles, reservations, and other parts of the business.

## What did you do particularly well?

I think I did particularly well at taking the client's requirements and organizing them into a clear system design. The UML diagrams helped show how customers, instructors, office staff, and administrators would interact with the system. I also considered important areas such as security, scheduling, user permissions, and preventing double bookings.

## If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?

I would improve some of the UML diagrams by adding more detail to the relationships and interactions between different parts of the system. This could make the design easier for another developer to understand and use when actually building the application.

## How did you interpret the user's needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user's needs when designing?

I looked at what each type of user needed to accomplish and designed the system around those tasks. Customers needed an easy way to schedule lessons and complete training, while employees needed tools to manage schedules, vehicles, and customer information. Considering user needs is important because a system can be technically successful but still fail if it does not solve the problems users actually have.

## How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?

I approach software design by first identifying the requirements and understanding the different users of the system. From there, I break the system into smaller processes and components and use diagrams to understand how they connect. In future projects, I would continue using requirement analysis, use cases, UML diagrams, and feedback from users before development begins. This helps identify problems early and gives the development team a clearer plan to follow.
